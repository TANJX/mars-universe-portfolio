# Intro — V2 Polish

A working doc for sharpening the intro copy. Goal: pull the language closer to the JD (`de-jd.md`) while keeping it personal and unforced.

---

## Diagnosis of V1

What's working:
- ¶2 ("AI-native experiences and the systems behind them") already mirrors JD pillars 1, 2, and 4 cleanly.
- The USC Iovine and Young line is uniquely yours and signals the design+engineering+product trifecta organically.
- Languages add personality at the end without trying too hard.

What's not working yet:
- **¶1's grammar is awkward.** "couldn't let go of either the opportunity to create something visual or build something meaningful" — the *either/or* construction makes the sentence read like you had to pick one. The point is the opposite: you wanted both.
- **No "taste" / craft / judgment beat.** The JD's third pillar — *bring taste to what ships* — is missing entirely. This is arguably the most distinctive thing about the role.
- **No POV statement.** Valleskey opens with a belief ("magic happens when product, design, and engineering work as one"). V1 doesn't have an analogous anchor — the reader gets your résumé before they get your stance.
- **Languages paragraph feels orphaned.** It's a fine note but currently sits as a full paragraph, which over-weights it.

What the JD calls for that V1 doesn't yet say:
- Builder mindset — *learning by making*.
- Prototyping — *concept to shipped*.
- Discernment — *what elevates the product experience*.
- AI-native belief — not just *what* you build but *why now*.

## Three Directions

Each option is a full draft. They make different bets about what to lead with.

---

### Direction A — Belief-first

*Lead with a POV, then ground it. Most Valleskey-shaped. Highest risk if the belief sentence misses; highest payoff if it lands.*

> **Mars Tan**
> Senior Design Engineer at InstaLILY AI.
>
> I believe the best software happens when design, engineering, and product stop handing off and start building together — and AI is making that more possible than it has ever been.
>
> At InstaLILY, I build customer-facing AI-native experiences and the systems beneath them: a design system that works as shared infrastructure for both humans and AI tools, and the internal workflows that turn emerging ideas into shipped product without losing taste along the way.
>
> I studied Arts, Technology, and the Business of Innovation at USC Iovine and Young — three lenses I still carry into every screen.
>
> English · 中文 · 日本語

What it's doing:
- Opens with belief; closes with origin. Inverts V1's order.
- "Without losing taste along the way" finally names the JD's third pillar.
- "Shared infrastructure for both humans and AI tools" is borrowed almost verbatim from the JD ("machine-consumable").
- Languages collapse to a one-line postscript.

What it costs:
- Risk of sounding manifesto-y. The belief sentence has to *feel* earned, not branded.
- "Three lenses I still carry into every screen" is poetic — fits Valleskey, may feel out-of-character if you're more matter-of-fact.

### Direction B — Tightened V1 (minimal surgery)

*Keep V1's order and voice. Fix the grammar, weave in JD vocabulary, demote languages.*

> **Mars Tan**
> Senior Design Engineer at InstaLILY AI.
>
> I studied Arts, Technology, and the Business of Innovation at USC Iovine and Young — a program built on the idea that good products live at the intersection of design, engineering, and human need. I haven't stopped designing for that intersection since.
>
> At InstaLILY I build customer-facing AI-native experiences and the systems beneath them: a design system as shared infrastructure, internal tooling that helps the team ship faster, and the considered details that make what's there feel intentional.
>
> English · 中文 · 日本語

What it's doing:
- Replaces V1's awkward *either/or* with "intersection of design, engineering, and human need" — same intent, cleaner grammar, JD-flavored.
- "Considered details that make what's there feel intentional" is the JD's third pillar in your voice.
- Drops the languages line to a thin postscript.
- Preserves the most of V1's rhythm.

What it costs:
- No POV / belief sentence — you stay descriptive, not declarative.
- Less distinctive than A or C.

### Direction C — Three-pillar (JD-mirrored)

*Name the role explicitly, then list the three things you do. Most JD-aligned, most professional, slightly more résumé-shaped.*

> **Mars Tan**
> Senior Design Engineer at InstaLILY AI.
>
> I build at the intersection of design and engineering — what some teams call design engineering, and what I think of as bringing taste to what ships.
>
> Day to day at InstaLILY, that means three things: shaping AI-native product experiences across chat, copilots, and agentic flows; building our design system as infrastructure that both humans and AI tools can build from; and shipping the internal tooling that helps the team move from concept to product faster.
>
> Before this, I studied Arts, Technology, and the Business of Innovation at USC Iovine and Young.
>
> English · 中文 · 日本語

What it's doing:
- Names "design engineering" explicitly — useful if you want recruiters and peers to land on this immediately.
- The three-pillar middle paragraph is near-direct JD vocabulary: *AI-native experiences*, *design system as infrastructure*, *internal tooling*.
- "Bringing taste to what ships" lifts the JD's exact phrase.

What it costs:
- More résumé-y. Less personal, more positioning.
- "Three things" framing feels structured — great for clarity, less for warmth.

## Open Sub-Decisions

1. **POV-first or origin-first?** A leads with belief; B and C lead with description. Pick the one that matches how you want the page to *feel* on first read.
2. **Name "design engineering" explicitly?** C does, A and B don't. Naming it helps SEO and immediate legibility; not naming it lets the work speak.
3. **Keep languages as a body paragraph or demote to a postscript line?** Recommendation: demote. It's a charming detail, not a thesis statement.
4. **Add the 日本語 → notes link?** It's a delightful inline easter egg in V1. Worth keeping in whichever direction you choose, just probably as a smaller postscript.
5. **Mention USC Iovine and Young by full name or shorthand?** Full name reads as more deliberate; shorthand ("USC Iovine and Young") is cleaner. Either works.

---

## My Recommendation

**Direction A**, with one caveat: rewrite the belief sentence in your own voice before committing. The structure (belief → what I do → origin → languages) is the strongest match for the Valleskey reference *and* the JD's "taste in engineering" thesis. B is the safe play; C is the recruiter play; A is the *designer* play — which is what this site is for.

If A's opening sentence won't sit right, fall back to B and don't overthink it.
