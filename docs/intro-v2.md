## Diagnosis of V1

What's working:
- ¶2 ("AI-native experiences and the systems behind them") already mirrors JD pillars 1, 2, and 4 cleanly.
- The USC Iovine and Young line is uniquely yours and signals the design+engineering+product trifecta organically.

What's not working yet:
- **¶1's grammar is awkward.** "couldn't let go of either the opportunity to create something visual or build something meaningful" — the *either/or* construction makes the sentence read like you had to pick one. The point is the opposite: you wanted both.

What the JD calls for that V1 doesn't yet say:
- Builder mindset — *learning by making*.
- Discernment — *what elevates the product experience*.

## ¶1 Variants Still Being Tuned

The two-sentence ¶1 is where the most work remains. Three close variants:

**Variant 1 — clean two sentences (current draft):**
> I've never wanted to choose between making things beautiful and making them matter. I think of design engineering as bringing taste into engineering.

**Variant 2 — closer to V1's vocabulary** ("visual" and "meaningful"):
> I've never wanted to choose between making something visual and making something meaningful. I think of design engineering as bringing taste into engineering.

**Variant 3 — more declarative belief** (drops "I think of"):
> I've never wanted to choose between making things beautiful and making them matter. Design engineering, to me, is what that looks like as a job — bringing taste into engineering.

**Variant 4 — single em-dash sentence** (more compact):
> I've never wanted to choose between making things beautiful and making them matter — which is why I think of design engineering as bringing taste into engineering.

Calls to make:
1. *"beautiful and matter"* vs *"visual and meaningful"* — rhythm vs. fidelity to V1.
2. Two sentences vs em-dash fusion — separation lets the belief land harder; fusion reads quicker.
3. *"I think of design engineering as..."* (hedged) vs *"Design engineering brings taste into engineering"* (stake in the ground).

## Discarded Directions (kept for record)

### Direction A — Belief-first (manifesto opener)
Read as too generative. *"I believe the best software happens when design, engineering, and product stop handing off and start building together — and AI is making that more possible than it has ever been"* is a formula sentence with no specific author. Anti-X / pro-Y construction, vague time-claim, no observation behind it.

### Direction C — Three-pillar JD-mirrored
Too résumé-shaped. The "three things at InstaLILY" framing is structurally clear but loses warmth. JD vocabulary lands harder when it's woven into prose, not listed.
